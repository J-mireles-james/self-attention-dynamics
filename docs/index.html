From Low-Dimensional Chaos to High-Dimensional Dynamics in Self-Attention Systems

J. D. Mireles James · Florida Atlantic University · November 2026

What changes when the particle models behind self-attention are allowed to move beyond the gradient setting? We investigate periodic orbits, torus-like recurrence, and numerical evidence of chaos—and how these behaviors interact when small systems are coupled into larger networks.

A theoretical and numerical investigation of idealized continuous-time attention systems. The observed chaotic dynamics are numerical findings, not computer-assisted existence proofs.

01 / Research problem

Beyond the consensus picture.

Under certain symmetry assumptions, projected self-attention has a gradient structure. This provides a powerful account of consensus, clustering, and metastability—but excludes nonconstant periodic orbits.

Here the query, key, and value matrices are constant yet not required to satisfy those symmetry assumptions. The resulting dynamics can be markedly richer, even for three particles constrained to an ellipse.

02 / Selected findings

Small systems. Unexpected complexity.

Analytic results and carefully qualified numerical observations, from a three-dimensional torus to a network of twenty interacting groups.

A · Analytical

An explicit periodic orbit

The fully synchronized three-particle configuration gives a periodic orbit whenever the effective matrix has nonreal eigenvalues. Its nontrivial Floquet multipliers can be computed in closed form.

Periodic, torus-like, and chaotic regimes

For other constant matrices, parameter scans reveal attracting nonsynchronized periodic orbits, candidate torus dynamics, changes of Floquet stability, and positive finite-time Lyapunov exponents.

03 / Reproducibility

Follow every experiment.

The public MATLAB package contains one driver script for each of the nine figures and two numerical tables, together with the supporting functions and archived data.

By default, drivers use archived numerical results. Set recompute = true inside a driver to repeat its calculation. Long parameter scans and the 60-particle Lyapunov computation can be expensive.
